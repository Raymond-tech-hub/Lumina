<h1>What is Osmosis?</h1>

Osmosis is the passive movement of water molecules across a semi-permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration. 

This process occurs <highlight=yellow>naturally</highlight>and does not require energy because it is driven by the concentration gradient of water. 

In biological systems, <highlight=blue>osmosis is essential for maintaining the balance of water within cells and tissues.</highlight> 
For example, in plant cells, water entering the cell causes it to swell and become turgid, which helps support the plant’s structure. 
Conversely, water leaving the cell in a hypertonic environment causes the cell to shrink, a process known as plasmolysis. 
In animal cells, which lack a rigid cell wall, water moving into the cell can cause it to burst, while water leaving the cell causes it to shrink or crenate.
Osmosis is also fundamental in various real-world applications, such as in kidney dialysis, food preservation using salt or sugar, and regulating water absorption in plants. 
Overall, osmosis is a critical mechanism for the distribution of water in living organisms and plays a central role in cell survival and function.
